# Researching the client and their trade

Do this for every demo, before designing. The call tells you what they asked for. Research tells you who they are
and how their work really runs. A demo built from the call alone reads like a summary of the call.

## Three things to learn

1. **Them.** Their site end to end, not the homepage only: about, services, products, branches, team, careers,
   FAQ. Their Google reviews, their Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn pages, news about them. What they sell and to
   whom, how big they are, what they are proud of, what their customers praise and complain about, the words they
   use for their own things.
2. **Their trade.** How does a business of this kind run a day? Which documents does it live on (a work card, a
   delivery note, a certificate, a policy, a quote)? Which standards and rules does it answer to? Which systems do
   such businesses already use, and what is known to be painful in them? What do professionals call things? Read
   until you could hold a short conversation with someone who works there.
3. **Their customers.** Who contacts them, through which channel, with which questions? What does a good answer
   sound like in this trade? An agent in the demo is judged on exactly this.

Search the web for each and fetch the pages. Prefer their own pages; when sources disagree, their official site
wins. Fifteen minutes of real reading is worth more than an extra screen.

## What it turns into

- **Recognition**: a real product of theirs, a real branch, their own word for a status, on the first screen.
- **Domain detail nobody asked for by name**: the form a regulator requires, the code on a label, the document that
  must go out with every job.
- **The look** (`design.md`): their colours, their photographs, the materials of their trade.
- **The agent's knowledge** (`demo-agent`): their hours, services, areas and policies as their customers ask them.

## Their brand

Download their logo and useful photographs into the app's `assets/` folder, so the demo does not depend on their
site. Take their colours and type from their real pages. If they have no usable images, draw neutral visuals; never
use stock photographs in place of a real business's own.

## What a public fact may and may not do

A public fact is used as a fact ("the site lists three branches"). It never becomes a private number: do not
present revenue, sales, stock, staff counts or performance as theirs unless their own source says so. Seeded
operations data is a believable world, and the spec says it is seeded. Record what you used and where it came from
in `demo-spec.json` (`publicSources`).
